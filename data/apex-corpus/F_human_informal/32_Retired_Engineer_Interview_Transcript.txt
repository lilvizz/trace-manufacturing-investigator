APEX THERMAL COMPONENTS -- KNOWLEDGE CAPTURE PROGRAM
Interview Transcript (lightly edited for clarity)

Subject: Dr. N. Ramachandran (retired 2019, formerly Process Engineer,
         Chennai Plant 2, 1997-2019)
Interviewer: Priya Ramesh, HR Knowledge Capture Coordinator
Date: 02-MAR-2024
Location: Phone interview
Purpose: General institutional knowledge capture, retired senior staff
         (part of ongoing program, not specific to any single equipment
         question)

---

PRIYA: Thanks for making time for this, Dr. Ramachandran. We're just
trying to capture some of the history from your years here before it's
lost. Can you tell me a bit about the Press 7 line, the thermal curing
one?

RAMACHANDRAN: Oh yes, Press 7. I spent a lot of time on that one in the
early days. That was the PX-200 machine, very early in my career here.

PRIYA: I understand there was some kind of temperature issue with that
machine?

RAMACHANDRAN: Yes, yes. This was 2001, if I remember right. We had
some alarm events, the temperature was overshooting during the long
runs. I did a whole characterization study on it -- ran trials at
different temperatures, found that above about 42 degrees the
controller started struggling to keep it stable during extended runs.
Something to do with the heating element response time in one of the
zones, zone two I believe.

PRIYA: And what happened after that?

RAMACHANDRAN: We set a new limit. I want to say... 42.3? Something
like that. It's been so long. The idea was to give some margin below
where things started going unstable, but not so conservative that it
hurt the cycle time too much. I remember there being some discussion
about whether to just round down to 40, but I felt that was more
conservative than the data supported.

PRIYA: Do you remember why 42.3 specifically, rather than a rounder
number?

RAMACHANDRAN: [laughs] Honestly, at this point, not exactly. I know it
was tied to the trial data somehow -- the trials were at whole and half
degree marks, 42, 43, and so on -- so there was some margin added on
top of the highest stable point. But whether it was exactly this
calculation or that one, I couldn't swear to it today. There should be
a note I wrote around that time, if it's still in the files.

PRIYA: The plant later replaced that machine, I believe? The PX-400?

RAMACHANDRAN: Yes, 2011 I think, after I'd moved partly onto other
projects by then. I wasn't heavily involved in that swap.

PRIYA: Do you know if the temperature limit was reviewed again after
the new machine went in?

RAMACHANDRAN: You know, I honestly don't know. I have a vague memory
of someone -- might have been Padmanabhan, he took over that area for a
while -- doing some kind of follow-up study on the new machine. But I
couldn't tell you what came of it, whether anything was changed. I was
mostly off on the Line 12 commissioning by then and didn't follow the
Press 7 story closely after that. You'd want to ask him, or check
whatever's on file from around 2014, if my memory of the timing is even
right.

PRIYA: That's very helpful, thank you. Anything else about that
equipment you think is worth recording?

RAMACHANDRAN: Just that it was a good learning experience for me, that
project. Taught me not to trust a vendor's rated range without doing
your own extended trials. That lesson stuck with me the rest of my
career here.

PRIYA: Thank you very much, Dr. Ramachandran.

--- End of Transcript ---
