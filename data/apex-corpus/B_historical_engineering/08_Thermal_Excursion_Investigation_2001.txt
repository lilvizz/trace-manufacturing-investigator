APEX THERMAL COMPONENTS -- CHENNAI PLANT 2
THERMAL EXCURSION INVESTIGATION REPORT

Report No: INV-2001-014                                Date: 30-JUL-2001
Prepared jointly by: Dr. N. Ramachandran (Process Engineering),
                      P. Sundaram (Quality Manager)

1. BACKGROUND
Two high-temperature alarm events were recorded on Press 7 (PX-200) during
extended production runs:
  - WO-01-0142, 22-APR-2001, batch 4471-4479
  - WO-01-0198, 02-JUN-2001

Both events occurred during continuous runs exceeding 5 hours, with alarm
triggers at cure-zone temperatures above the then-interim 45.0 deg C
setpoint threshold being approached (peak recorded excursions of 44.6 C
and 45.3 C respectively). No mechanical fault was identified by Maintenance
(see WO-01-0143).

2. RELATED STUDY
This investigation follows and is informed by the Process Engineering
characterization study (PE-STUDY-2001-07, 05-Jul-2001), which independently
identified thermal instability developing above approximately 42 deg C
during extended runs, attributed to zone-2 heater element response lag.

3. ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS
The field alarm events are consistent with the instability mechanism
identified in PE-STUDY-2001-07: as continuous run duration increases,
zone-2 controller correction response degrades, permitting temperature
excursions above setpoint that grow larger with sustained operation.
The interim 45.0 deg C operating boundary set at installation (1999,
based on a standard 10% derate from vendor rated maximum) does not
account for this time-dependent degradation and provides insufficient
margin for extended production runs.

4. RECOMMENDATION
Recommend Quality and Process Engineering establish a revised, more
conservative upper operating boundary for the cure zone that:
  (a) provides adequate margin below the onset of instability observed
      in the 42-43 deg C region (per PE-STUDY-2001-07), and
  (b) accounts for realistic extended-run durations representative of
      full production shifts (6+ hours), not just short-duration testing.

A cross-functional review was held (Quality meeting, 14-Jun-2001; see
minutes ref QM-2001-06) to discuss these findings and agree in principle
on a conservative revised boundary. Process Engineering to issue a
follow-up engineering note specifying the final numeric limit, once the
supporting margin calculation is finalized. This investigation report is
being formally issued following that discussion, incorporating feedback
from the meeting.

5. INTERIM ACTION
Pending the revised limit, Production has been instructed (verbal,
Production Supervisor M. Elango, 25-Apr-2001) to limit continuous run
duration to 4 hours where cure-zone target exceeds 40 deg C, until a
formal limit is issued.
