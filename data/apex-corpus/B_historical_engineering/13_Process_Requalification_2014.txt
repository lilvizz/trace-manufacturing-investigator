1. BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVE
Since PX-400 was qualified in 2011 as an equipment-swap within the
existing 36.0-42.3 deg C process envelope (see QUAL-2011-021), production
volumes on Press-07 have increased and cycle-time optimization has become
a business priority. This study was commissioned by the Manufacturing
Engineering group to characterize PX-400's actual thermal stability
envelope, independent of the legacy PX-200-derived limit, to inform
potential future cycle-time optimization work.

Note: This study is a characterization exercise. It does not itself
constitute an approval to change the production operating limit; any
limit change would require a separate ECO per standard change control
(QP-002 Section 7).

2. METHOD
Extended trials (6-8 hr continuous runs, representative of full
production shifts) were conducted on PX-400 at target cure-zone
temperatures of 40, 42, 42.3 (current UCL, as control), 43, 44, and
45 deg C, using the same part quality acceptance criteria as the
original 2001 PX-200 study for comparability.

4. DISCUSSION
Unlike the 2001 PX-200 characterization (PE-STUDY-2001-07), which showed
controller overshoot and increasing instability above ~42 deg C due to
zone-2 heater response lag, the PX-400 unit's RTD sensor array and
HPS-CTRL v3.2 controller (with faster PID response and improved thermal
mass distribution) show NO comparable instability through at least 44 deg
C, and no controller-level instability even at 45 deg C. The modest
increase in blister rate observed at 45 deg C appears to be a part-quality
effect (likely related to polymer degradation kinetics at that
temperature) rather than a control-stability issue, and is a materially
different failure mode from the 2001 finding.

The PX-400 platform did not show a comparable instability onset within
the range tested here. However, this study characterizes control-loop
and short-to-medium-term part quality behavior only; it does not address
longer-term material or equipment effects, nor does it constitute a
revalidated production specification.

5. CONCLUSION
PX-400 demonstrates stable thermal control across a broader range
(through at least 44 deg C) than the current 42.3 deg C production UCL,
under the trial conditions tested. This is presented as engineering
characterization data for future consideration. It does not, in itself,
change SOP-CURE-07 or the SPC configuration, and no such change is
proposed as part of this study.

6. RECOMMENDATION
If cycle-time optimization via an expanded operating window is desired
in future, this data would support scoping a formal limit-change ECO
(including updated SPC configuration, SOP revision, and a full
justification memo per QP-002 Section 7). No such ECO is being raised
at this time; this study is filed for reference.
