PROCESS ENGINEERING STUDY REPORT

Report No: PE-STUDY-2001-07                            Date: 05-JUL-2001
Title: Thermal Curing Process Characterization -- Press 7 (PX-200)
Prepared by: Dr. N. Ramachandran, Process Engineer
Reviewed by: K. Doraiswamy, Plant Engineering Manager

1. OBJECTIVE
Following two years of production on Press 7 (PX-200), this study was
commissioned to characterize the cure-zone thermal profile across an
extended operating range, in support of finalizing production operating
limits (the original 1999 installation used an interim upper limit of
45.0 deg C pending this study -- see Installation Record PE-INST-042).

2. METHOD
Extended production trials were run at target cure-zone temperatures of
36, 38, 40, 42, 43, and 44 deg C, each sustained for a minimum of 6 hours
of continuous operation (representative of a full production shift),
with part quality assessed at 30-minute intervals.

4. DISCUSSION
The data indicate that thermal stability of the PX-200 cure zone begins to
degrade above approximately 42 deg C during extended (multi-hour) production
runs, likely related to controller response lag combined with heater element
thermal mass at zone 2. Below 40 deg C, no instability was observed across
the full trial duration. The interim 45.0 deg C limit set at installation
does not appear supportable given the overshoot and quality effects observed
at 43-44 deg C.

5. RECOMMENDATION
Recommend Quality and Engineering jointly review these findings to establish
a revised production operating boundary that provides adequate margin below
the onset of observed instability (approximately 42-43 deg C region). See
forthcoming investigation report for root-cause detail on the April and
June 2001 field alarm events (WO-01-0142, WO-01-0198), which appear
consistent with the borderline behavior observed here.
